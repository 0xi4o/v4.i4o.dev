# The funnel and the channel map

Every question in SaaS marketing hangs off one picture. Keep this page; every
lesson refers back to it.

## The funnel

Five stages. A person moves down them, and people leak out at every step.

| Stage          | Plain English                                       | The question it answers                |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Awareness**  | They hear you exist                                 | "How do I get users?" starts here      |
| **Interest**   | They visit your site                                | Is the landing page earning attention? |
| **Signup**     | They create an account                              | Conversion — visitors who act          |
| **Activation** | They reach the moment the product is useful to them | Did they experience the point of it?   |
| **Revenue**    | They pay                                            | Only meaningful once the above work    |

Two consequences worth memorising:

1. **Work the funnel bottom-up.** Traffic into a page that doesn't convert, or
   signups into a product nobody activates in, is wasted effort. Fix the later
   stages first, with a handful of users, before pouring people in at the top.
2. **Every marketing activity maps to exactly one stage.** If you can't say
   which stage an activity feeds, you don't know why you're doing it.

## The channel map

Channels are just ways of creating awareness. Two families:

**Organic (costs time, compounds slowly)**

- Direct outreach — messaging individual people. The only channel that works
  from zero. Where everyone starts.
- Communities — Reddit, Hacker News, Discord/Slack groups, forums. Being
  usefully present where your users already talk.
- Content marketing — writing things your users want to read. Slow to start,
  compounds for years.
- SEO — making that content findable via search. A multiplier on content, not
  a separate thing.
- Social — X, LinkedIn, video. Builds an audience that sees everything you ship.
- Launches — Show HN, Product Hunt. One-off spikes, not a strategy.
- Word of mouth — users telling other users. Earned by the product, amplified
  by making it easy to share.

**Paid (costs money, stops when you stop paying)**

- Search ads, social ads, sponsorships (newsletters, podcasts, YouTube).
- Rule of thumb for this course: paid is off the table until organic has proven
  people want the product and you know what a user is worth. Money spent
  earlier mostly buys lessons you could have had free.

## Which channel when

| Users you have | Where your effort goes                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0 → 10         | Direct outreach, by hand ([Graham](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html), [Rachitsky](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-businesses)) |
| 10 → 100       | Communities + a launch + early content ([PostHog's account](https://posthog.com/founders/first-1000-users))                                                    |
| 100 → 1,000    | Content + SEO compounding; social; word of mouth                                                                                                               |
| 1,000+         | Consider paid, now that you have data                                                                                                                          |
