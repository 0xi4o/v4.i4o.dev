You asked nine questions — users, channels, analytics, SEO, content, and
"where do I start?". Good news: the last one has a known answer, and once you
see the map, the other eight fall into an order. This lesson gives you the map
and your first move for Kobun. Nothing else. The rest of the course works
through the map one piece at a time.

## The map: a funnel

Every SaaS user, everywhere, goes through the same five steps. They **hear you
exist** (awareness), they **visit your site** (interest), they **sign up**,
they **reach the moment the product becomes useful to them** — marketers call
this <Term word="activation" plain="the first genuinely useful moment, e.g. a user publishes their first page through Kobun" /> —
and eventually they **pay**.

People leak out at every step. A thousand people can hear about Kobun, thirty
visit, three sign up, one activates. That leaky pipe is called the

<Term word='funnel' plain='wide at the top, narrow at the bottom' />, and it's the whole map. Every
marketing question you listed lives at one spot on it:

- "How do I get users?" and "what channels are there?" → the **awareness** end.
- "How do I convert them?" → **interest → signup**.
- "How do I track what users do in my app?" → **signup → activation**.
- SEO and content marketing → awareness channels, ones that pay off slowly and
  then keep paying.

Two rules follow from the shape, and they're the ones beginners miss:

**Rule 1 — fix the bottom before pouring into the top.** Driving traffic to a
landing page that doesn't convert, or collecting signups nobody activates from,
wastes the scarcest thing you have. With a handful of users you can find and
fix those leaks cheaply.

**Rule 2 — every activity must name its stage.** "I should post on X" is not a
plan. "I'm posting on X to create awareness among SSG users" is. If you can't
name the stage, skip the activity.

The full one-page version, including the organic-vs-paid channel map, is in the
reference: [The funnel and the channel map](../reference/the-funnel-and-channel-map.mdx).
Print it if that's your thing.

## Your first move: recruit users by hand

Here's the part that feels wrong to an engineer, which is exactly why it needs
saying early. At zero users, the only channel that works is **talking to
individual people, one at a time**. Not SEO (months to work, and you need
content first). Not ads (money spent learning things you can learn free). Not
even a big launch (a spike into an untested funnel — see Rule 1).

This isn't a beginner's compromise; it's how nearly everyone did it. Paul
Graham's ["Do Things That Don't Scale"](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html) is the
canonical argument — the Airbnb founders went door to door in New York. Lenny
Rachitsky [studied how over twenty B2B companies](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-businesses)
(Figma, Notion, Airtable…) found their first ten customers: personal networks
and going where users already gather, in almost every case. And
[PostHog — an open-source devtool, the closest thing to Kobun's situation —
got their first thousand](https://posthog.com/founders/first-1000-users) via
Hacker News and word of mouth, not ads.

Manual recruiting also quietly solves a problem you didn't list: at this stage
every user is also a research subject. Ten hand-picked users who talk to you
teach you more than a thousand anonymous signups.

<Callout label="This week's task — about an hour">

Open a note and write two things:

1. **One sentence:** who Kobun is for and the problem it removes. Format:
   "Kobun is for **[specific person]** who **[situation]** and want
   **[outcome]**." A working guess is fine — lesson 2 will pressure-test it
   properly.
2. **A list of ten.** Real people you could message directly (former
   colleagues, dev friends who blog, anyone who's complained to you about
   CMSes), and real places your users already talk (specific subreddits,
   Discords, forums — names, not categories). This list is lesson 3's raw
   material. Don't message anyone yet.

Bring both back to our next session.

</Callout>
