Source: [Nygard, Documenting Architecture Decisions (2011)](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions).
Variants: [adr.github.io](https://adr.github.io/) (MADR adds *Decision
drivers* and *Considered options*).

## When to write one

One decision, made once, that a future engineer would otherwise ask "why on
earth?" about. A design doc covers a system; an ADR covers a choice. Write one
for decisions made inside a design doc that deserve to be findable on their
own, and for decisions made without a design doc at all.

## The five sections

| Section | Nygard's words | Yours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Title** | "short noun phrases" | Number and the decision: `0001 — Store docs as Markdown with a SQLite index` |
| **Context** | "the forces at play, including technological, political, social, and project local" — value-neutral | What was true, what was pulling in which direction. No opinions. |
| **Decision** | "our response to these forces" — active voice, "We will…" | One paragraph. The choice, stated against what it was chosen over. |
| **Status** | proposed → accepted; later deprecated or superseded by NNNN | One word, plus a link when superseded. |
| **Consequences** | "the resulting context, after applying the decision. All consequences should be listed here, not just the 'positive' ones" | What is now easier, what is now harder, what you have to do because of it. |

Length: "one or two pages". Prose "as if it is a conversation with a future
developer"; "bullets are acceptable only for visual style, not as an excuse
for writing sentence fragments".

## File conventions

- One directory in the repo: Nygard uses `doc/arch/adr-NNN.md`; the common
  modern layout is `docs/adr/NNNN-short-title.md`. Pick one per repo and
  never move it.
- Numbers are sequential and never reused.
- A reversed decision is not deleted or edited. Write a new ADR, mark the old
  one *superseded by NNNN*, and link both ways.
- Keep an index (a `README.md` in the directory, or the ADR table in
  `career/notes.org`): number, title, status, one-line decision.

## Self-review before committing

- Is the title a noun phrase naming the decision, not the problem?
- Could a stranger read the context and not know which way you went?
- Does the decision sentence say what it was chosen over?
- Do the consequences include at least one thing that got harder?
- Is there anything in it that belongs in the design doc instead (how it
  works, rather than why it was chosen)?
- Is it under two pages?

## Where other formats differ

- **MADR** ([adr.github.io](https://adr.github.io/)) adds *Decision drivers*
  (the forces as a list) and *Considered options* with pros and cons per
  option. Use it when the alternatives matter more than the narrative.
- **Y-statements** (also on adr.github.io) compress an ADR to one sentence:
  "In the context of X, facing Y, we decided Z to achieve A, accepting B."
  Good as the one-line entry in the index.
