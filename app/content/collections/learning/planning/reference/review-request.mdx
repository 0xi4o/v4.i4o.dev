## The form — the five lines at the top of the doc

```
Status:     Reviewing solution            (WIP | Reviewing problem | Reviewing solution | Approved | Abandoned)
Wanted:     Challenge the storage and manifest decisions (§3, §4). Not wanted: prose, naming.
Decider:    <you>                         Approvers: <1–2 people who must say yes>
Input from: <2–3 people whose experience you want>     FYI: <anyone affected>
Closes:     <date, 5–10 working days>     Comment in: <the doc / the PR / the thread>
```

## Why each line

**Status** tells reviewers what kind of reading to do. "Reviewing problem"
means check the context and goals and ignore the design; "Reviewing
solution" means the goals are agreed and the design is open. Reviewing both
at once produces comments that cancel each other.

**Wanted / not wanted** is the line most people skip and the one that most
improves the comments. Name the sections and the decisions. Say what you do
not want so nobody spends their time on it.

**Decider / approvers / input** separates "must agree" from "I would like
your view". One decider. Approvers are the people who can block; keep it to
the people who actually can. Everyone else is input or FYI.

**Closes** is the Rust final-comment-period idea: a date after which silence
is consent. Without it a review never ends.

## Triage, when the comments arrive

Sort every comment into one of five kinds before replying to any of them:

| Kind | Looks like | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Challenge** | "Why not X?" where X is a real alternative | Answer in the doc (design or alternatives), not only in the thread; link the thread. |
| **Gap** | "What happens when…?" | Add to the design, or to open questions with what would resolve it. |
| **Evidence** | "Have you measured…?" | Point to the POC, or add it to next steps. Never argue; measure. |
| **Preference** | "I'd have used Y" with no goal violated | Acknowledge, say which goal decided it, do not change the doc. |
| **Nit** | Typos, wording, naming | Fix silently. No reply needed. |

Reply to challenges and gaps in the doc first, then in the thread with a
one-line pointer. Reviewers read the doc, not the thread, a month later.

## Closing

Add a short **Review outcome** section at the end: what changed and why,
what was raised and not changed and why, and the status change ("Approved
2026-10-20 by A and B"). Then broadcast one message — HashiCorp's pattern —
to everyone on the FYI line: what was decided, link to the doc. The review is
over when that message is sent, not when the comments stop.

## Self-review before sending the request

- Is there exactly one decider?
- Does "wanted" name sections and decisions, and does "not wanted" exist?
- Is there a close date?
- Are the diagrams and the ADRs linked from the doc, so a reviewer has one
  entry point?
- Is the doc in the state the status claims? (A "Reviewing solution" doc with
  an unfinished design section wastes everyone's week.)
