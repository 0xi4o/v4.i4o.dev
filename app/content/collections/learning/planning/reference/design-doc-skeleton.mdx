The skeleton every doc in this course uses. Sources:
[Ubl, Design Docs at Google](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/);
[Slatton, How to write a good design document](https://grantslatton.com/how-to-design-document).

## Should you write one?

Three questions ([Ubl](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/)).
Any yes means write it; three noes means build it.

1. Are you unsure of the right design?
2. Would a senior engineer's input change it?
3. Is it contentious enough that agreement matters?

## The five sections

| Section | The question it answers | One-pager budget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Context and scope** | What does a reader need to know to follow the rest? Facts only. | 60–100 words |
| **Goals and non-goals** | What must be true when this ships? What might a reader expect that we are leaving out? | 3–5 bullets each |
| **Design** | What are we building, and what did it cost us? Choice → reason → cost → open question. | 150–250 words, one sketch |
| **Alternatives considered** | What else would have worked, and which trade-off lost? | 2 options, ~40 words each |
| **Cross-cutting concerns** | What would security, privacy, observability or on-call ask first? | 30–60 words |

Optional, when they earn their place: a system-context diagram, an API sketch
(not a schema), a note on data storage, pseudo-code only for a novel
algorithm, and an **open questions** list at the end.

Length ([Ubl](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/)):
one to three pages for a feature or sub-task; ten to twenty for a large
project. Cut before you add.

## Goal or design choice?

Ask: *could a different design satisfy this bullet?* No → it is a design
choice; move it to the design section and write the outcome underneath it as
the goal. A goal should also be checkable without asking the author, and a
non-goal should surprise at least one reasonable reader and carry a clause on
why it is out. Collect open questions in their own section at the end, each
with what would resolve it.

## Ordering

[Slatton](https://grantslatton.com/how-to-design-document): arrange it so the
reader is never surprised. Each paragraph follows from the last; objections
are answered before the reader raises them; every paragraph can be summarised
in one sentence. Long calculations go in an appendix.

## Section-by-section tells

**Context** is wrong if it contains an opinion or a decision. Move those down.

**Goals** are wrong if they are design choices in disguise ("use Postgres" is
a decision, not a goal — the goal is what Postgres buys you).

**Non-goals** are wrong if nobody would have expected them anyway.

**Design** is wrong if a paragraph has no cost in it. "We chose X" needs
"over Y, because Z, at the price of W".

**Alternatives** are wrong if you would never have picked any of them. A real
alternative is one you would have accepted had the trade-off gone the other way.

**Cross-cutting concerns** are wrong if absent. Two sentences is enough for a
one-pager; zero is not.

## Self-review before sharing

- Could someone outside the meetings follow the context without asking you?
- Is every non-goal something a reader might reasonably have expected?
- Does every design paragraph name what you gave up?
- Is at least one alternative a real option?
- Can each paragraph be summarised in one sentence?
- Is there at least one open question?
- Is it one page?

## Where other processes differ

Noted here so the skeleton travels; taught in later lessons.

- **Rust RFC** ([template](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/master/0000-template.md))
  adds *Drawbacks*, *Rationale and alternatives*, *Prior art*, *Unresolved
  questions*, *Future possibilities*. The drawbacks section is worth stealing.
- **Kubernetes KEP** ([template](https://github.com/kubernetes/enhancements/blob/master/keps/NNNN-kep-template/README.md))
  adds named reviewers/approvers and a production-readiness questionnaire.
- **Oxide RFD** ([RFD 1](https://rfd.shared.oxide.computer/rfd/0001)) adds a
  state: prediscussion → ideation → discussion → published → committed →
  abandoned. "Abandoned" is a legitimate ending.
- **HashiCorp** ([writing practices](https://www.hashicorp.com/en/how-hashicorp-works/articles/writing-practices-and-culture))
  splits the problem (PRD) from the solution (RFC) into two docs.
- **Sourcegraph** ([handbook, archived](https://github.com/sourcegraph/handbook/blob/main/content/company-info-and-process/communication/rfcs/index.md))
  reviews the problem statement before the solution, with explicit status labels.
