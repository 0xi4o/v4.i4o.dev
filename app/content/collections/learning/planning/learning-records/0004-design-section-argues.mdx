# The design section argues — DocuDex doc is a complete first version

After lesson 3 every design paragraph in the DocuDex doc carries a choice
stated against what it was chosen over, a reason, a cost, an "I accept that
because" sentence and, where relevant, open questions. The alternatives
section mirrors the design choices (CLI vs hosted, server vs static site,
both storage formats vs one, manifest vs `go.mod`), and the storage paragraph
answers the cgo question (`modernc.org/sqlite`, for single-binary
distribution). "Why now" was added. Quiz 4/4; two self-reported misses in the
sorting drill, both on "reason only" vs "assertion only" — a distinction that
matters less than the one he got right (none of the paragraphs had a cost).

## Evidence

- Three drafts in ten days, each fixing what the previous review named, with
  one residual carried once ("via godoc") and then removed.
- Costs are now written, not just known; "I accept that because…" appears in
  every design paragraph.
- Residuals: the CLI paragraph's cost is really the serving design's cost;
  scope alternatives (scraping, TypeScript) still sit in Alternatives; the
  "why now" section ends in a puffery paragraph; end-of-doc open questions
  lack resolution lines.
- Reviewer content notes given: Markdown as source of truth with SQLite as a
  rebuildable index dissolves the sync cost; the "packages that use godoc"
  assumption is a misconception (API reference is extractable for every Go
  package via `go/doc`; the gap is narrative guides).

## Implications

- The one-page design doc skill is established. Stop teaching the skeleton;
  from here lessons change gear (ADRs, POC write-up, diagrams, review).
- Puffery in the "why now" section is the first sign of product-spec drift
  in the writing itself; a one-line nudge, not a lesson.
- Lesson 4 (ADRs) uses two decisions already argued in this doc — storage and
  manifest — so the ADR is a compression exercise, not new thinking.
