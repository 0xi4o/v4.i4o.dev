# Baseline — bare nodes, strong Linux and Terraform, coming in blind on networking and hypervisors

Ilango starts with three bare M710qs (8 GB, SATA SSD each, NVMe in two), a
TL-SG2008 still in its box, and a home network he has not mapped. He is
fluent on the Linux CLI and uses Terraform and AWS at work. He asked, after
seeing a first draft that opened with "choose a hypervisor", for everything
to be taught from first principles: he knows "a little" about homelabs,
networking, Proxmox, DHCP and VLANs and wants the fundamentals of each thing
before doing it. Chosen shape: infra first, apps as payoff, one hands-on
evening a week.

## Evidence

The first draft of lesson 1 assumed subnets, DHCP pools and hypervisors as
known vocabulary. He said so and asked for first principles. Treat that as
the floor: nothing about networks, virtualisation or clustering is assumed.

## Implications

- Do not teach shell, ssh, Git or Terraform syntax. Teach every networking
  and virtualisation concept the moment it is first needed, with a concrete
  test he can run on his own network.
- Concept before action, every lesson: a short first-principles section,
  then hands-on that uses only what was just taught. No lesson may lean on
  a term that has not been earned in an earlier lesson or the reference.
- The rewritten lesson 1 is the home network (addresses, subnet, router,
  DHCP, DNS) with the rack only placed on paper. The switch (lesson 2)
  and the hypervisor decision plus install (lesson 3) come after.
- Every lesson changes something real (a map, the switch, a node) and adds
  to `RUNBOOK.md`.
- The 8 GB per node constrains VM sizing until the RAM upgrade lands.
